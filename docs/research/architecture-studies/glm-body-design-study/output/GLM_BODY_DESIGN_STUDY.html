GLM-style attention, varied model body

87 controlled architecture variants · ~1–10T capacity anchors; main5T/10T designs · GB300 and Rubin proxy · September2026

September9 extension: Internal attention dimensions and coordinated scale-ups adds58 variants and corrects decode index-reuse accounting. This page retains the older formula and results. Its0.7 efficiency scenario should no longer be read as a validated calibration for the corrected formula.
Question: with the attention design fixed, which costs come from expert activation, expert granularity, stored capacity, shared experts and sequential depth? These are inference-performance comparisons, not predictions of model quality or of proprietary architectures.

What is fixed and what changes

All variants use GLM DSA top2048,64 attention heads, fixed Q/KV low-rank and head dimensions, index reuse at the native four-layer frequency, and FP8 KV. Most controlled comparisons keep residual width6144 and depth78. When residual width changes, the attention input/output projections change too; when depth changes, the same attention block is repeated more times. Attention algorithm and inner dimensions are fixed, not total attention work.

Every request has262,144 cached tokens. Decode has no prefill work. The prefill supplement adds exactly1,024 new tokens. Routed experts use NVFP4; non-expert GEMMs use BF16 uniformly across variants. This consistent synthetic policy is not an exact copy of every native checkpoint's per-layer quantization exception. Model totals below are parameter-count estimates excluding small normalization/bias terms; targets refer to approximate routed-parameter budgets.

Hardware: inherited GB3008TB/s HBM, Rubin22TB/s; directional fabric0.9/1.8TB/s; Rubin dense compute proxy, not adaptive headline FLOPS. Memory admission uses90% of modeled capacity. The64-GPU budget is tiled with16/32/64-GPU worker groups. TP1/2/4/8 is searched; independent attention groups=world/TP. Mixed TP+DP uses AIC's SGLang analytical graph because its TRT-LLM selector rejects that layout; TP1 uses TRT-LLM. Mixed layouts are less directly grounded than the measured pure-attention-DP anchor.

Main charts are SOL. A separate0.7 efficiency scenario is provided, not silently applied. Speculation is explicitly a four-proposal/AL3.36 scenario borrowed from the earlier DSpark study, not a measured GLM acceptance forecast. The native calibration used three-proposal MTP; its efficiency must not be assumed invariant under this change.

Principal findings

  • Active parameters are not a sufficient decode-cost predictor. For~5T at EP64 and4 requests/rank, moving K128→K8 reduces estimated active parameters from396B to42B, but improves GB300 decode only90→112 TPS/user (1.25×). At1 request/rank the improvement is104→187 (1.79×). At a global batch of256, K8 already touches about70% of1728 experts in expectation; high K touches almost all. Attention remains unchanged.
  • Expert granularity is a smaller SOL effect in this regime. With exactly matched routed total and active budgets and no shared experts, M1024→M8192 improves GB300's four-requests/rank rate about91→97 TPS/user (7%). This does not establish equal real kernel efficiency for tiny and large experts.
  • Depth/width can move performance much more. The~5T L48/H4096 geometry yields122 TPS/user versus93 at L78/H6144 and51 at L128/H12288, at fixed EP64 and4 requests/rank. Routed budgets are approximate, not exact:128-wide expert rounding can move capacity several percent; inspect manifest totals before treating a pair as strictly parameter-matched.
  • Capacity-only scaling is cheaper than proportional-active scaling. At K8,~5T→~10T changes112→98 GB300 TPS/user. Keeping K/E near1/32 changes92→60. Capacity-only scaling still loses throughput because weight reuse falls and cache headroom shrinks.
  • Rubin improves absolute rates but does not remove these tradeoffs. At the same fixed four-requests/rank shape, the native-like~5T and~10T cases give about250 and164 TPS/user, compared with92 and60 on GB300. These near-bandwidth-ratio gains are SOL projections, not measured Rubin efficiency.
  • Faster memory need not increase target-feasible concurrency when capacity already binds. With the assumed speculation, the native-like~5T GB300 case reaches704 requests at96 TPS/user; Rubin reaches640 at271. The lower modeled Rubin count comes from the inherited conservative decimal288GB capacity assumption versus GB300's driver-visible277.55GiB, not a demonstrated real hardware capacity regression. In this regime bandwidth mainly buys faster users. Consult no-spec and0.7 scenarios separately.

How to interpret the mechanisms

  • Expected distinct expert reads follow E×[1−(1−K/E)^T], where T is tokens participating in the routed step. The model does not assume every token activates disjoint experts until exhaustion.
  • At low load, more/smaller experts can increase distinct destinations and router work even when active parameter FLOPS match. At high load, most expert weights are already touched; expert granularity then mainly changes kernel shape and communication. Real small-expert kernel penalties are not fully represented by SOL.
  • Reducing K lowers active expert work but leaves attention and other always-active work intact. Its benefit must plateau when those terms dominate.
  • Reducing depth removes sequential attention, projection and communication work; the budget-preserving larger experts add parallelizable work instead. This is not free model quality: training behavior and reasoning capability are not modeled.
  • Increasing E at fixed K can still increase expert-weight traffic across a batch because reuse falls, and it consumes memory that could hold more requests. Fixed active parameters do not guarantee fixed inference cost.
  • Shared experts are always active; they are replicated or sharded differently from routed experts. Their memory/bandwidth costs can matter even when their parameter share is small.

Confidence and boundaries

The empirical GLM anchor was native-model GB300 attention-DP8, roughly10 live requests/rank and heterogeneous~163K mean context. It supported about0.69× SOL with a padding-aware approximation. This study changes capacity, EP size, expert shapes, depth, width, context and speculative width. Therefore0.7 is a scenario, not a newly validated efficiency coefficient. Rubin has no measured anchor here.

Quality is not held constant. The lowest-active, shallowest design is not automatically the most plausible frontier model. Shared-expert count, expert fragmentation, top-K router behavior and trainability may impose constraints not visible to AIC. Treat the fixed-ratio/native-like expert-width cases as more conservative architectural baselines and the very low-K/very shallow cases as aggressive performance hypotheses.

Routing uses the existing expected-occupancy analytical extension and includes modeled communication; it is not a fully calibrated rank-skew, kernel-startup or topology-contention simulator. Memory ceilings assume balanced placement. Sparse attention input/output projection dimensions are updated with H; head geometry and index selection remain fixed. Parameter counts include router, shared/dense MLP, embeddings and attention estimates but omit small terms.

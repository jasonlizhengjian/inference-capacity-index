Mechanism

The plateau is mathematically expected

Once the token-proportional term dominates a decode step, doubling the batch doubles both useful tokens produced and elapsed step time. Those factors cancel in aggregate throughput.

In the high-concurrency region, the model is well approximated by a fixed component plus a component proportional to concurrency C:

TPOT(C) ≈ a + bC
rack TPS(C) ≈ 64,000C / (a + bC)Therefore rack TPS → 64,000 / b as C becomes large.

The fitted high-concurrency slope is 0.194 ms/c-r on GB300 and 0.125 ms/c-r on Rubin. This produces fitted ceilings of approximately 330K and 511K rack TPS.

More capacity still increases the count of resident requests. It simply cannot prevent their individual generation rates from approaching zero as the fixed rack budget is shared more finely.

Diminishing returns

Rubin saturates earlier—and higher

The bars show how much of each fitted asymptotic throughput ceiling has already been reached. Reaching the ceiling is good for utilization, but it also means extra concurrency mostly becomes extra latency.

Rubin is already at about 96% of its fitted ceiling by c/r 64 and effectively saturated by c/r 128. GB300 is around 82% at c/r 64 and approaches its lower ceiling more gradually.

This is why Rubin can support many more agents at 50 or 100 TPS/user, but cannot support arbitrarily many at those rates. After saturation, each doubling of concurrency produces close to a halving of TPS/user.

The Rubin/GB latency ratio also compresses—from 2.65× at c/r 1 to 1.56× at c/r 2,048—because the extreme-batch slope includes token-proportional work that is not accelerated by the nominal HBM-bandwidth ratio alone.

Interpretation

Five conclusions that survive the thought experiment

These conclusions are about the model’s structure and the shape of the SOL envelope. Production values require an efficiency discount and serving-system validation.

01

“Maximum concurrency” needs an SLO

Memory-full concurrency is 191 c/r here, but the useful boundary is only 35/79 c/r at 100 TPS/user or 87/159 c/r at 50 TPS/user for GB300/Rubin.

02

Capacity-only scaling has weak late returns

Past 191 c/r, extra memory admits more 256K contexts but adds little rack throughput. It is a resident-state benefit, not a proportional serving-capacity benefit.

03

Bandwidth raises the ceiling

Rubin’s meaningful advantage is its higher throughput envelope: about 511K versus 330K modeled rack TPS. This permits more concurrent agents at the same per-user rate.

04

Equal-concurrency speedup is not constant

The gain is largest when fixed weight-streaming pressure dominates. It shrinks at extreme batches as token-proportional compute, KV/attention work, and communication determine the slope.

05

“Fill memory” is not a universal policy

At memory-full c/r 191, Rubin provides 41.8 SOL TPS/user and GB300 25.3. Whether either is reasonable depends on the agent’s responsiveness target.

06

Rubin is close to full utilization earlier

For throughput maximization, Rubin needs far less than its full resident capacity. The remaining memory headroom is useful for context residency, burst absorption, or isolation—not more steady-state tokens.

What is being held fixed?

The four models receive equal weights. Across hardware, we use 256Ki context, nextn3/AL3 for decode, a 1Ki-new-token prefill objective and the same workload input/output ratio. The index adds the modeled accelerator requirements of separate prefill and decode pools, not an arithmetic blend of phase scores. Queueing and tool-wait duty cycles are excluded.

All results are analytical SOL projections, not measured serving performance. MAI-2 2.8T combines disclosed architecture with explicitly assumed dimensions and speculative decoding; it is not an exact Microsoft production implementation.

2. Capacity per rated accelerator watt

For NVIDIA, AMD, Google TPU, Microsoft Maia, Meta MTIA and OpenAI Jalapeño. A value of 2 means twice GB300’s modeled index capacity per rated watt, using GB300’s 1,400 W rating.

Power-normalized score = capacity score × 1,400 W / accelerator watts

This is a rated-power proxy, not measured energy efficiency. TDP, module power and SoC power have different boundaries. External switches, CPUs, cooling and facility overhead are not added. The same rating is charged to every allocated prefill/decode accelerator; we do not model workload-dependent power or power-cap performance.

Power sources and the Ironwood estimate

Epoch AI’s machine-learning hardware dataset, retrieved October 15, 2026, supplies TDP for the matched NVIDIA profiles, MI355X, Maia 200 and TPU v7. We use only its power column; we do not replace any frozen timing or hardware-spec inputs.

Ironwood’s 960 W is an Epoch estimate inferred from Google’s relative FLOP-per-watt claim: (4,614 / 46) × 280 / 29.3 ≈ 959 W, rounded to 960 W. It is not an official absolute Google TDP.

For entries absent from Epoch, we retain sourced study values: Jalapeño 700 W; Maia 300 950 W SoC TDP; MTIA 400/450/500 at 1,200/1,400/1,700 W module power. Both MTIA 500 memory scenarios use the same roadmap power assumption. Maia’s SoC boundary and other vendors’ module/package boundaries are not proven identical.

Power-normalization divides the already computed index by a hardware-specific constant. It does not select different configurations or claim the modeled peak bandwidth and FLOPs are simultaneously sustainable at that rating.
